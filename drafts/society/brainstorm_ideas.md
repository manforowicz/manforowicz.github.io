Thoughts I'd like to talk about:

- We can have a free market AND wealth redistribution. They're not at odds with each other. I think equality is necessary for a democratic free market. Democracies arose during the industrial revolution when ordinary people got more wealth and were able to demand rights.

- A lot of humans are genuinely greedy. If there's something to be exploited, it will be exploited. How can we harness this greed? If government is too strong, greedy politicians might do bad things. So capitalism imposes laws saying that companies are powerless to order others around, unless there are voluntary transactions. This aligns incentives - the only way to get wealthy is by convincing others to give you money by offering things they want.

- Historically, everyone was born with inherent capital - their muscle and brains. This is like nature's UBI. They can rent them out to others. Mechanization made muscles worthless, and AI will make brains worthless. Therefore, wealth will only be driven by how much you already have. Since nature's UBI is gone, we need our own UBI. Also, voting itself is a form of societal UBI since it gives everyone 1 vote of power.

- Almost all market failures are due to externalities. These are things that someone didn't agree to. I believe externalities can also happen within one person. The impulsive part decides to gamble, which imposes an externality on the long term thinking part which didn't agree to gamble. This mental model justifies internalizing these externalities by taxing alcohol and gambling.

- Subsidies and taxes are a form of intervention that very visibly show how large their impact is in dollars, and allow for clear discussion of tradeoffs. On the other hand, regulations (minimum parking requirements, height limits) are very hidden. They're just a sentence on a piece of paper. But if a government tried to build the parking themselves, or tax height, it would become apparent how impactful and significant these are. For example, I think NYC granted height limit exceptions to developers that built plazas, rather than simply paying for the plazas themselves - this is more politically doable, but obscures true cost. Sometimes laws are necessary. Imposing a tax on dumping mercury into a river might mean an evil billionaire could dump some. Also, it'd be hard to figure out how much someone dumped. Instead, we should make a law which is a bit like an infinite tax.

- Subsidizing not buying cigarettes and taxing cigarettes have the same effect. We should tax (as it gives revenue) unless it's administratively expensive (like taxing everyone who doesn't innovate).

- Countries like Norway and China export more than they import. So they give away more goods and services than they get in return. They get intangible assets like bonds in return. They hope to use these to flip the direction of trade in the future. What prevents the US from declaring their bonds obsolete? This would make other try to sell these bonds, lowering their price. So to sell new bonds, the US would have to offer a very high interest rate on them. Demand for dollars to buy US bonds drops, so the US dollar depreciates. This prevents the US from importing, which defies its whole goal.

## Designing a stable government

- Let's design a system that's resilient to human greed.

- Every time a leader doesn't listen to citizens, they could storm the government and put in a new leader.
    - But this is messy. What if it's actually a small group of rowdy people that stormed the government instead of a majority? What if it causes a lot of violence and uncertainty?
    - Elections are a more predictable way to kick out bad leaders. But what if the leader decides not to listen to elections? To avoid a messy uprising, we need a designated person or group of people to kick out the leader.
    - But how do we ensure that designated person isn't friends with the bad leader? We set up a prisoner's dillema situation. The designated person receives significant reward if they follow the social contract.
    - But who's there to give that reward? We need another designated person. In fact, let's create a cycle of designated people. And thus, we have checks and balances.
    - We also reward a branch from reporting or breaking up unofficial coalitions between other branches, to make the system self replicating.
    - If that all fails, citizen revolt is a final backstop, but risky because a few citizens could gain power, and not reimpose a checked-and-balanced system.
