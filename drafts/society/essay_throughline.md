# Ethics


## Ethics module
- Evolution hardwired human brains to be greedy.
- Greed involves punishing free-riders.
- Greed incentivizes us not to be a free-rider.
- Evolution hardwired ethics into brains.
- There is no universal ethical truth because our ethics modules are adaptive and wishy-washy.
- The best we can do is enjoy life and do whatever "feels right".

## Generalizing to larger civilizations
- Our ethics module evolved in small settlements, where free-riders would be directly punished.
- But how can we punish free riders in large modern societies, when we interact with new people every day? For example, if one neighborhood kicks out a troublemaker, they can just go make trouble in another neighborhood.

## Stable government design
- Let's designate some officials whose sole job is to tell people which troublemakers to detain.
- But why should people listen to what an official says? Social contract: we obey officials, and they will give commands to detain troublemakers.
- How can an official stay in power? By ordering people to punish anyone who doesn't listen to them. Self-fulfilling prophecy and stable equilibrium.
- What if an official gives orders that people don't like? A critical mass has to abandon the social contract. Some people will keep following the social contract out of habit, and they must be fought off.
- Modifying the social contract often requires some violence. This is risky and haphazard, and a worse social contract may emerge.
- To avoid violence when switching officials, let's use a social contract that allows for official changes within a single social contract via voting. This is typically a constitution.

## Command economy

- Let's say any goods produced by you are evenly distributed to everyone by the state.
- Anyone who purposefully takes more than their fair share is punished by the state.
- What motivates you to produce goods if most of them are distributed across society?
- Everyone will want to take the easy jobs like movie star. There's nothing to incentivze them to take other jobs.
- So the state steps in to assign jobs to people. If someone consumes shared goods but refuses to work, they're punished by the state. This is hard to enforce, as people can pretend their working. So the state will need to be powerful, with a system of inspectors, etc.
- Powerful state leads to a risk of abuse of power.
- How does the state decide which jobs to assign people to? How do we choose the optimal quantities of soccer balls, doors, tomatos, and bicycles to produce? What if we produce too much food one year, and to little another, causing a famine?
- Having the state coordinate and manage everyone is difficult, and error prone. Let's try something more decentralized.
